A few questions

There is no rush and there are no wrong answers. What you write here is what makes the difference between a website that looks like yours and one that looks like everybody’s. Answer in whatever language you prefer.

One

Your work

As you would explain it to someone at dinner who has never heard of a closet concierge. Your words here will become the words on the site.
The visit, the wardrobe edit, the shopping, the seasonal rotation — whatever your process is. The more concrete it is, the less daunting it feels to book.
And is there one you would like to be known for above the others?
Two

Your client

Age, where she lives, what her life looks like. If you already have one or two real clients in mind, describe them — far more useful than a general description. And since you want to work internationally: which cities beyond Zürich do you have in mind?
This decides whether the contact form simply collects messages or quietly filters them before they reach you. There is no wrong answer — but it changes the design.
One word is enough. Calm, discretion, precision, warmth, authority — whatever is true for you.
Three

Discretion & images

In your profession this matters more than in most. Tell me where the line is and I will design within it.
The single most important image on the site. People are engaging you, not a company — and it has to be a genuine photograph.
Images of you working, wardrobes you have organised, details, clients’ pieces. Anything at all — even phone photographs help me judge what is missing.
The monogram and the typography from your card. If you have the original files, please send them; if not, a clear photograph of the card is enough to begin.
Four

Taste & details

They need not be in your field — a hotel, a gallery, a jeweller. This tells me more about what you want than any other question here. And if there is one you dislike, that is just as useful.
You mentioned you want to reach an international clientele. If English leads, the whole site is conceived in English and the others are written beside it — which reads very differently from a German site with a translate button.
Something you have seen elsewhere and want to avoid, a word you never want on your site, an idea you have been carrying for a while. Anything.

Your answers come directly to me and are used only for this project.
